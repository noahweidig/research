# Variability in the management of agricultural fields spared for conservation

## Abstract

Private land conservation initiatives are primary channels for wildlife population recovery and management in agricultural ecosystems. Billions of dollars are allocated to these programs in the United States and Europe. Ensuring cost-efficiency is a top priority for funding agencies and substantial resources are dedicated to targeting enrolment to maximize environmental benefits. Landowner management of conservation fields can moderate environmental benefits and may undermine cost efficiency if done incorrectly, therefore it is important to accurately monitor and predict landowner compliance to contract specifications. We investigated landowner compliance to one key wildlife-friendly recommendation (“conformance”) and the amount of woody encroachment in a grassland conservation program, the Kentucky Conservation Reserve Enhancement Program. We used two methods to estimate conformance to wildlife-friendly mowing recommendations and woody encroachment: aerial surveys and supervised classification of satellite imagery. We fit a double observer binomial model to helicopter survey data to estimate impacts of landscape features (e.g., distance to road, field size) on mowing intensity and woody cover. We used the random forest algorithm in Google Earth Engine (n trees = 1000) to classify vegetation management from SPOT-6 and SPOT-7 satellite images. We surveyed 306 fields in February 2016 via helicopter and classified images from November 2015. At the field scale, estimates of mowed and woody vegetation from image classification were similar to observations from helicopter surveys (<7% difference on average). Approximately 70% of contracts were conformant to wildlife-friendly recommendations. Most variation in mowing intensity and woody encroachment was random. Landscape features were not sufficient predictors of field management and landowners operated independently of one another. Our study is a potent example of how the mis-management of conservation parcels by landowners may diminish efforts to maximize environmental benefits. Resource management agencies that administer private land conservation should dedicate considerable resources to ensuring landowners follow best management practices. Using remote sensing technology is a viable method for surveying compliance at programmatic scales.
