# Climate-driven ponderosa pine dieback and regionally variable pathogen pressure across the western United States

## Abstract

Ponderosa pine ( Pinus ponderosa ) is a foundational species in dry-forest landscapes across western North America. Ongoing warming and aridification intensify climatic stress across its native range. Prior research on ponderosa pine health is regional in scope and focuses on single disturbances, leaving the combined influence of climate, stand structure, and biotic agents on dieback and mortality poorly understood. To address these knowledge gaps, we surveyed sites across California, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, and Wyoming during 2024 and 2025. At each site, we characterized stand conditions, assessed tree health, and collected tissue samples for pathogen identification. Long-term changes in climate were also recorded at each site to test associations between tree health and biotic and abiotic factors. Recent climate trends included reduced precipitation, elevated mean temperatures, and changes in vapor pressure deficit, indicating the potential for climatic stress on trees. Of the 324 trees surveyed, 25.8% had detections of pathogens and symptoms consistent with pathogen infection. Pathogens were composed of two functional groups: (1) needle and branch pathogens, including the widely distributed endophyte, Sydowia polyspora , primarily associated with trees of moderately poor health status, and (2) vascular stain fungi that were strongly linked to recent mortality. Only S. polyspora showed significant relationships with abiotic conditions in generalized linear models, while non-metric multidimensional scaling revealed clear geographic structuring of pathogen assemblages. In summary, our study suggests that climatic stress is associated with increases in host vulnerability to biotic agents, while fungal pathogens exhibit distinct associations with moderate decline and recent mortality.
