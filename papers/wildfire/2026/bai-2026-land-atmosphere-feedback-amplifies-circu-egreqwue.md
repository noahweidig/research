# Land-atmosphere feedback amplifies circulation-dominant fire weather risk over eastern Siberia

## Abstract

Wildfires in eastern Siberia have intensified significantly in recent decades. This intensification is closely linked to rising vapor pressure deficit (VPD), a key indicator of fire weather risk. Previous studies have shown that atmospheric circulation increases VPD through subsidence-induced warming and drying, but the extent to which the circulation-driven VPD increase is realized through land–atmosphere feedback remains unclear. Here we show that atmospheric circulation explains about 60% of the 2004–2024 summer VPD trend over eastern Siberia, while circulation-triggered land-atmosphere feedback provides a major amplification pathway, accounting for about 42% of the circulation contribution. Mechanistically, reduced Laptev sea ice concentration favors a regional anomalous anticyclonic circulation over eastern Siberia, suppressing precipitation and depleting soil moisture. The resulting soil drying shifts surface energy from latent toward sensible heat, further amplifying regional VPD. These findings reveal a key land-surface pathway through which circulation intensifies fire weather risk and provide a broader framework for understanding similar changes in other fire-prone regions. Atmospheric circulation dominates the increase in fire weather risk over eastern Siberia in the past two decades, with circulation-triggered land-atmosphere feedbacks amplifying about 42% of that effect, as suggested by attribution of vapor pressure deficit based on reanalysis data.

Wildfires are a natural disturbance of terrestrial ecosystems, yet rapid climate warming is reshaping fire regimes across many regions by enhancing fire-favorable weather conditions<sup>1–4</sup> . Recent satellite observations reveal unprecedented fire activity near the Arctic Circle, highlighting the growing importance of boreal wildfires for air quality, carbon storage, and climate<sup>2,5–7</sup> . Because boreal ecosystems store vast amounts of carbon in forests, permafrost, and peatlands, intensified burning can release disproportionate carbon and potentially reinforce regional warming and drying through a positive climate-fire feedback<sup>5,7–9</sup> . These developments underscore an urgent need to better understand the physical drivers of recent wildfire intensification across boreal ecosystems. 

Eastern Siberia is a major hotspot of boreal wildfire activity<sup>10,11</sup> . In recent decades, wildfire occurrence in this region has increased markedly, accompaniedbyrisingcarbonandpollutantemissions<sup>11–13</sup> .Inparticular,the 2021 fire season generated the largest regional CO₂ emissions of the 2000−2021 record<sup>5</sup> . Recent studies have identified eastern Siberian boreal forests as a top-down-dominated region, where wildfire activity is influenced more strongly by weather precursors than by fuel precursors<sup>14</sup> . Among these weather-related precursors, vapor pressure deficit (VPD), an indicator of atmospheric aridity, has emerged as a leading metric for 

investigating the climatic controls on wildfire intensification because it integrates the combined effects of temperature and humidity on atmospheric dryness<sup>12,15</sup> . Consistent with this, VPD has also been shown to be closely linked to burned area (BA) and fire-related carbon emissions over eastern Siberia<sup>5,12,16</sup> . 

VPD variability from synoptic to decadal time scales is strongly controlled by large-scale atmospheric circulation<sup>17–20</sup> . Persistent anticyclonic or high-pressure systems favor hot and dry conditions by enhancing subsidence and suppressing precipitation, thereby increasing VPD<sup>19,20</sup> . Because eastern Siberia is one of the regions with relatively strong land-atmosphere coupling during the warm season in the Northern Hemisphere<sup>21–23</sup> , soil moisture (SM)–VPD feedbacks, hereafter referred to as land-atmosphere feedbacks (LAF), may also play an important role in modulating regional VPD<sup>24</sup> . Soil drying suppresses evapotranspiration, reduces atmospheric moisture, and thereby increases VPD. In turn, elevated VPD enhances atmospheric evaporative demand and further depletes SM<sup>20,24,25</sup> . However, the influences of atmospheric circulation and LAF on VPD are intertwined because circulation-induced SM variability provides the land-surface preconditioning necessary for subsequent LAF amplification<sup>24,26,27</sup> . Here, we quantify the contributions of atmospheric circulation and circulation-triggered LAF to the summer VPD trend over eastern Siberia during 2004 − 2024 using flow analog and partial regression methods. Overall, these analyses provide a processbased attribution of the increasing fire weather risk over eastern Siberia, highlighting the important role of land-surface feedbacks in amplifying circulation-driven climate extremes. 

## Results 

### Escalating wildfire activity associated with rising fire weather risk 

The intensity of wildfire in eastern Siberia described by BA and carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions exhibits a pronounced seasonal cycle, with more than 82.3% of annual BA and 87.8% of annual CO2 emissions climatologically occurring in June–August (JJA) during 2002–2022, the common period over which both fire indicators are available (Supplementary Fig. 1). Although BA begins to increase more prominently than CO₂ emissions in late spring, this discrepancy may reflect lower emissions per unit area burned during early-season fires, because CO₂ emissions depend not only on burned area but also on fuel load, fuel consumption, combustion completeness, and burning depth. Nevertheless, the dominance of both BA and CO₂ emissions in JJA supports our definition of JJA as the core fire season for investigating recent wildfire changes over eastern Siberia. 

Eastern Siberia stands out as a hotspot of rapidly intensifying wildfire activity, coinciding with deterioration in meteorological fire weather conditions. Spatial trend maps exhibit that the strongest increases in BA and CO2 emissions across northern high latitudes are concentrated over eastern Siberia, with pronounced coherent upward trends in fire weather metrics, particularly VPD and the fire weather index (FWI) (Fig. 1a–d). The spatial patterns of increasing VPD and FWI closely resemble those of BA and CO₂ emissions, indicating that the recent escalation of wildfire activity is tightly linked to worsening fire weather. This linkage is further supported by correlation analysis between fire activity and fire weather. Regional mean anomaliesreveal a sharp rise in VPDafter the early 2000s, with a statistically significant trend of 0.67 hPa decade⁻¹ over 2004–2024, which is consistent with previous studies<sup>12,13</sup> (Fig. 1d, g). Importantly, VPD co-varies strongly with multiple independent fire indicators during 2002–2022, including BA (r = 0.80; p < 0.01) and CO₂ emissions (r = 0.80; p < 0.01), and shows an even tighter relationship with FWI (r = 0.92; p < 0.01) (Fig. 1g), underscoring that VPD provides a robust indicator of recent fire weather intensification over eastern Siberia. 

As a key meteorological variable, VPD is a very important factor that influences fuel aridity and fire behavior<sup>28,29</sup> . Previous studies have highlighted that persistent ridging or blocking over boreal Eurasia can create favorable fire weather conditions via high air temperature and reduced precipitation<sup>12</sup> , which can further reduce SM content and enhance atmospheric aridity through land-atmosphere interactions<sup>24</sup> . During 2004–2024, the observed VPD increase over eastern Siberia is accompanied bya marked upward trend in sea level pressure (SLP) and a widespread decline in SM (Fig. 1d–f), suggesting that anomalous circulation and SM depletion may jointly contribute to the recent increase in VPD. However, it is unclear whether anomalous circulation can also influence SM and thereby further amplify VPD through LAF. If so, to what extent does circulation-triggered LAF associated with SM depletion contribute to the recent VPD increase? 

### Attribution of the VPD trend over eastern Siberia 

Using SLP anomalies as the circulation proxy, we apply a circulation analog method to quantify the contributions of atmospheric circulation to the observed summer VPD trend over eastern Siberia during 2004–2024 (see Methods). To minimize the contamination of long-term warming and drying signals in the analog reconstruction, we first remove the 1950–2024 linear trends from SLP, SM, and VPD at each grid point. All attribution analyses below are then performed using the detrended residual fields. The observed VPD trend over eastern Siberia decreasesonly modestly from 0.67 to 0.55 hPa decade⁻¹after removingthelong-termtrend (Fig. 2a), suggesting that the recent increase is primarily driven by internal climate variability rather than global warming. The reconstructed circulation-induced VPD trend is estimated to be 0.33 hPa decade⁻¹ (Fig. 2a), accounting for approximately 60% of the observed VPD trend. This indicates that anomalous high-pressure circulation is the dominant driver of the recent VPD increase. It is noted that the reconstructed SLP trend closely matches the observed pattern (Fig. 2a, b), and the estimated circulation contribution is insensitive to reasonable choices of circulation domains and sampling parameters (Supplementary Fig. 2a–d), supporting the robustness of the analog reconstruction. 

Recent studies have shown that high-pressure circulation anomalies can induce soil drying through suppressing precipitation and increasing temperature, thereby further amplifying VPD through LAF<sup>30,31</sup> . To isolate the VPD component associated with soil-moisture variability and thereby assess whether the total circulation effect contains an amplification realized through the SM pathway, we first apply a SM analog approach to diagnose thecontributionofLAFtotheobservedVPDtrend.ThereconstructedLAFinduced VPD component shows a significant upward trend of 0.24 hPa decade⁻¹ (Fig. 2c), corresponding to approximately 44% of the observed VPD trend. Sensitivity tests also yield consistent estimates (Supplementary Fig. 2e, f), suggesting that the inferred LAF contribution is robust to the choiceof sampling parameters. This result demonstrates a pronounced SMmediated amplification signal underlying the recent VPD increase. 

To further diagnose whether the total circulation effect contains an amplificationrealizedthroughcirculation-triggeredLAF,wedefineregional VPD_SLP and VPD_LAF indices by averaging the reconstructed circulation-induced and LAF-induced VPD components over eastern Siberia during 1950–2024. The VPD_SLP and VPD_LAF indices covary strongly, with a correlation coefficient of 0.56 that passes the 99% significance test (Supplementary Fig. 3a). In addition, the VPD_SLP index exhibits a significant negative relationship with SM over eastern Siberia (Supplementary Fig. 3b), indicating that anomalous anticyclonic circulation increasingVPDtend to co-occurwith soildryingovereasternSiberia.These results show a substantial overlap between circulation-induced and LAFinduced VPD components, suggesting that part of the total circulation contribution to VPD is realized through LAF associated with circulationinduced SM depletion. 

We then use a partial regression method (see Methods) to isolate this overlap component by decomposing the circulation-induced VPD trend into (i) a direct circulation contribution (ε) and (ii) a circulation-triggered LAF contribution (k × VPD ~~L~~ AF). This decomposition shows that the direct circulation contribution is 0.19 hPa decade⁻¹ (Fig. 2d), with positive VPD trends mainly concentrated over central and eastern Siberia and closely aligned with the core of the fitted SLP trend pattern, while the circulation-triggered LAF contribution reaches 0.14 hPa decade⁻¹ (Fig. 2e). Thus, circulation-triggered LAF accounts for roughly 42% of the total circulation-induced VPD increase, highlighting a substantial land amplification of dynamical forcing. 

It is notedthat this separation relies on a linear approximation between circulation and SM. Given the inherently nonlinear land-atmosphere interactions in the real world, the linear removal of SM effects cannot be perfect. Nevertheless, after removing the effect of LAF, the regression of SM onto the VPD_SLP index is markedly weakened relative to the unadjusted case (Supplementary Fig. 3b, c), indicating that the procedure effectively isolates the dominant overlap component.

## Discussion 

This study provides a process-based attribution of the recent intensification of fire weather risk over eastern Siberia. Based on multiple independent fire indicators, we show that spatiotemporal variability of VPD closely matches that of BA and fire-related carbon emissions, supporting the view that VPD is a robust and physically meaningful proxy for fire risk. Using the flow analogmethods,we findthatlarge-scalecirculationanomaliesexplainabout 60% of the observed VPD trend, while circulation-triggered LAF accounts for roughly half of the circulation-induced VPD trend, highlighting a substantial land-surface amplification of dynamical forcing. Mechanistically, the dynamical contribution is associated with a persistent high-pressure ridge linked to Laptev SIC anomalies, while circulation-induced soil drying enhances LAF by altering surface energy partitioning, thereby further amplifying atmospheric aridity. 

To quantify this amplification, we decompose the circulation-induced VPD trend into a direct circulation contribution and a circulation-triggered LAF contribution. This framework is physically plausible because, on seasonal timescales, atmospheric circulation generally acts as an upstream driver of soil moisture variability. Although the flow analog methods have been widely used to quantify the role of atmospheric circulation or SM in extreme events, a key caveat is that the impact of circulation on LAF is inherently nonlinear, so a linear removal of SM effects from the circulation contribution cannot be exact. ### Data treatment 

Summer monthly Fire Weather Index (FWI) data for 1979–2024 were obtained from the Copernicus Emergency Management Service for the European Forest Fire Information System (EFFIS) with horizontal resolution of 0.5° × 0.5° grid points<sup>35</sup> . Monthly BA and fire-related CO₂ emissions for 2002–2022 were obtained from the Global Fire Emissions Database, version5(GFED5)<sup>36</sup> .Monthlymean2-mairtemperature(T)anddewpoint temperature (Td), zonal and meridional winds (U, V), geopotential height 

(HGT), vertical velocity, sea level pressure (SLP), latent heat flux (LH), sensibleheat flux(SH),cloudcover,andnetshort-waveradiation flux(Swrf) were obtained from the fifth-generation European Center for MediumRange Weather Forecasts atmospheric reanalysis (ERA5) at 1.0° × 1.0° resolution<sup>37</sup> . Monthly surface SM in the upper 0–7 cm layer was derived from ERA5-Land at 0.1° × 0.1°<sup>38</sup> . Monthly precipitation data were obtained from the Global Precipitation Climatology Project (GPCP)<sup>39</sup> at 2.5° × 2.5°, and seaice concentration (SIC) data were taken from the Hadley Center Sea Ice and Sea Surface Temperature dataset (HadISST)<sup>40</sup> at 1.0° × 1.0°. All analyses were performed for boreal summer (June–August, JJA). Linear trends were estimated using least-squares regression.

### Flow analog method 

The flow (circulation and SM) analog method<sup>42–46</sup> is performed in this study to quantify the relative contributions of atmospheric circulation and LAF to the observed trend in summer VPD over eastern Siberia. The key idea is to estimatethe summerVPDvaluesbyconditioningonagivenseasonal-mean circulation (SLP) pattern and SM pattern from other years within a reference period (1950–2024) that exhibit similar patterns. Before the analog fitting and VPD reconstruction, we removed the 1950–2024 long-term trends from SLP, SM, and VPD at each grid point to minimize contamination from the background warming and drying trends. Here, we briefly summarize the algorithm. For each target year, we first randomly subsample (without replacement) 35 observedsummer SLP or SM anomaly patterns during the reference period (except for the target year) overeastern Siberia. Next, we reconstruct the SLP or SM patterns using an optimal linear combination of summer SLP or SM patterns from 35 randomly selected years based on multiple linear regression. We further apply the same coefficients derived from the optimal linear combinations of SLP or SM patterns to the corresponding VPD fields and obtain the circulationinduced or LAF-induced VPD patterns. The random selection and optimal reconstruction are repeated 100 times to avoid overfitting. Finally, we average the 100 reconstructed VPD fields to provide the best estimates for each target year. Therefore, the circulation-induced or LAF-induced VPD trend can be calculated using least squares regression analysis for all target years (2004-2024). Here, the VPD trend reconstructed from the SM analog method is used to represent the contribution of LAF to VPD trend because SM and VPD are closely coupled through land–atmosphere interactions<sup>24</sup> . 

To assess the robustness of the analog reconstruction, we conducted a series of sensitivity tests by varying the circulation domain and sampling parameters (Supplementary Fig. 2). For the circulation analog, two alternative circulation domains, including a smaller domain (57° − 75°N, 102° − 162°E) and a larger domain (52° − 75°N, 95° − 170°E) were used to select analogous circulation patterns (Supplementary Fig. 2a, b). The contributions from these two domains were comparable to our baseline choice (Fig. 2a), explaining approximately 64% and 58% of the observed VPD trend, respectively. We also tested different sampling parameters by randomly selecting 30 or 40 analogs, which yielded similarly estimated circulation contributions (Supplementary Fig. 2c, d). For the SM analog, different sampling parameter settings were likewise tested, and the resulting contributions remain quantitatively similar to the baseline estimate (Supplementary Fig. 2e, f). Therefore, our estimation of circulation and LAF-induced contributions are robust to reasonable choices of the circulation domain and sampling parameters. 

### Linear decomposition of the circulation-induced VPD component 

It should be noted that the circulation-induced and LAF-induced VPD components reconstructed by the flow analog methods are not fully independent, because circulation anomalies can induce SM anomalies that trigger LAF and amplify VPD. To quantify the fraction of the total circulation effect that is realized through this soil-moisture pathway, we further isolate the circulation-triggered LAF contribution using a partial regression method. 

Specifically, for each grid point, we regress the circulation-induced VPD component (VPD_SLP) onto the LAF-induced VPD component (VPD_LAF) across the target years:

Here, k × VPD LAF denotes the fraction of the circulation-induced VPD that covaries with the LAF-induced VPD, which is interpreted as the circulation-triggered LAF contribution. The residual term, ε, represents the direct circulation contribution that is independent of land feedbacks. Therefore, the circulation-induced VPD component is further separated into a direct circulation contribution (ε) and a circulation-triggered LAF contribution (k × VPD LAF). The linear trends of these two components during 2004–2024 are then calculated to quantify their respective contributions to the observed increase in summer VPD over eastern Siberia. 

### CMIP6 models 

To investigate the role of land-atmosphere interactions in amplifying the recent summer VPD trend over eastern Siberia under the same circulation conditions, we analyze the Land Feedback Model Intercomparison Project with prescribed Land Conditions (LFMIP-pdLC) experiment in Land Surface, Snow and Soil Moisture Model Intercomparison Project (LS3MIP)<sup>47</sup> , SSP5-8.5 experiment in ScenarioMIP<sup>48</sup> , and the historical experiment provided by Coupled Model Intercomparison Project phase 6 (CMIP6). The forcing in LFMIP-pdLC experiment during 2004–2014 is the same as the historical experiment, while the forcing for the period of 2015–2024isconsistent with theSSP5-8.5.IntheLFMIP-pdLCexperiment, SMisprescribedasthemonthlyclimatologyduringtheperiodof1980–2014 from historical global climate model output. Then the contribution of land–atmosphere interactions can be estimated through the difference between the fully coupled experiments (historical and SSP5-8.5 experiments) and LFMIP-pdLC experiment. 

Since there are six CMIP6 models that participate in LS3MIP and provide the required variables, we first evaluate each model’s ability to reproduce the observed spatial pattern of summer VPD climatology over eastern Siberia using a Taylor diagram, which jointly measures the correlation coefficient, root-mean-square difference, and standard deviations. Models with the high correlation and few errors are considered to better reproduce the observed VPD climatology. Therefore, EC-Earth3, MIROC6, and MPIESM1-2 show relatively poor performance and are excluded from further analysis (Supplementary Fig. 6a). Second, among the remaining models, we further require that the observed dynamical linkage between VPD and SLP anomalies (i.e., the VPD response under similar circulation conditions) be realistically reproduced. Although IPSL-CM6A-LR performs reasonably well in reproducing the climatological spatial pattern of VPD, it fails to reproduce the expected VPD response under similar circulation conditions and is therefore excluded from the primary analysis (Supplementary Fig. 6b). The final analysis thus focuses on CESM2 and CMCC-ESM2, which best reproduce both the VPD climatology and the key SLP-VPD relationship, providing a more robust basis for assessing the amplification effect of landatmosphere interactions. 

We also apply the circulation analog method separately to the CP and PS experiments from the CESM2 and CMCC-ESM2 models to quantify the effect of LAF under similar large-scale circulation conditions. Specifically, foreachsummerduring2004–2024,analogcirculation fieldswereidentified from the CMIP6 simulations over the reference period 1980–2024 after removing the trends based on their similarity to the observed circulation anomalies, and the corresponding VPD fields were then reconstructed underanalogouscirculationconditionsineachexperiment.Theirdifference (CP minus PS) was taken as the amplification effect of LAF on the VPD trend under similar circulation conditions.
