# Exceptionally high temperatures in Armenia in 2023: orographic effects, snowpack response and synoptic drivers

## Abstract

Climate conditions in 2023 were exceptionally warm globally. The mean global near-surface temperature in 2023 was ranked as the second warmest since 1850, while the warmest year was observed in 2024 1 . The mean temperature anomaly in 2023 was 1.45 ± 0.12 °C compared to the 1850–1900 average 2 , 3 . The jump in global mean annual temperature in 2023 relative to 2022 (0.31 °C) has been the strongest over the last 143 years 4 . Sea surface temperatures (SSTs) significantly contributed to the warming in 2023. Warm SST anomalies were observed at multiple ocean basins at the same time 5 . The transition from multi-year La Niña conditions to El Niño conditions observed in 2023 5 , 6 , 7 likely contributed to the strong warming of SSTs over the globe. SST anomalies exceeded 1.5 °C over the tropical North Atlantic and the western tropical Indian Ocean. The warm SSTs and air temperatures substantially reduced the global sea-ice extent and the sea ice around Antarctica in 2023 7 . Europe experienced a record number of days with “extreme heat stress” in 2023 8 . Extremely high temperatures affected the United States and Mexico in July, and the maximum temperature reached 53.3 °C in Death Valley 9 . Overall, 42% of the globe experienced exceptionally warm conditions in 2023 3 . Widespread wildfires were observed over Canada and Greece 2 , 10 , 11 . The deadliest Storm Daniel over the Mediterranean caused thousands of deaths in September, while damages exceeded $20 billion 12 . The formation of Storm Daniel was partly attributed to anomalously high SSTs in the Mediterranean observed during summer 2023.

Climate conditions in 2023 were exceptionally warm globally. Armenia experienced exceptionally high temperatures in March, November and December 2023, resulting in an extremely low mountain snowpack. Advective processes associated with meridional (in March) and zonal (November-December) flows were dominant largescale factors producing strong positive temperature anomalies across Armenia. Mountain topography both dampened and enhanced positive temperature anomalies through formation of nocturnal cold-air pooling and stronger daytime radiative heating, respectively. 

Climate conditions in 2023 were exceptionally warm globally. The mean global near-surface temperature in 2023 was ranked as the second warmest since 1850, while the warmest year was observed in 2024<sup>1</sup> . The mean temperature anomaly in 2023 was 1.45 ± 0.12 °C compared to the 1850–1900 average<sup>2,3</sup> . The warm SSTs and air temperatures substantially reduced the global sea-ice extent and the sea ice around Antarctica in 2023<sup>7</sup> . 2023wasalsoanomalouslywarmforArmenia;itwasthethirdwarmest year since 1935, while March and November were the warmest on record<sup>13</sup> . Armenia is a mountainous and landlocked country located in the mid- 

latitudesoftheNorthernHemisphere(SupplementaryFig.1).Thecountry’s average elevation is 1800 m above sea level (a.s.l.), with altitudes ranging from 375 to 4090 m a.s.l. (Mount Aragats). The mountain topography produces10altitudinalclimaticbeltsacrossArmenia,rangingfromaridand continental lowland conditions to high-mountain tundra climate<sup>14</sup> . The combination of a continental climate and mountainous terrain makes the country particularly vulnerable to climate change<sup>15,16</sup> . Caucasus Mountains located in the study region experienced strong warming in recent decades<sup>17</sup> . Furthermore, Armenia is surrounded by the two climate-change “hot spots,” theMediterraneanandMiddleEast<sup>18–20</sup> .Substantialwarming<sup>21,22</sup> and more frequent heat waves<sup>23–25</sup> affected Armenia in recent decades. The axis of the Eurasian subtropical jet, which is known to produce significant climate anomalies and weather extremes including strong winds, heatwaves, droughts and floods<sup>26,27</sup> , passes over Armenia along 40°N latitude. In fact, a range of climate hazards have impacted Armenia over recent decades<sup>28–31</sup> . Themountaintopographymayfurtherinducelocalclimatehazards,suchas extremely cold temperatures due to the formation of local and persistent cold-air pools<sup>32</sup> or heavy rainfall induced by orographic convection<sup>33</sup> . 

The climate hazards associated with present and near-future climate change can significantly impact the mountain ecosystems of Armenia, emphasizing the importance of studying various climate anomalies over the study area. This study focuses on the impacts of exceptionally high temperatures across Armenia that occurred in 2023. We further seek to understand the orographically driven temperature anomalies, that is, the presence of orographically induced either enhanced or suppressed temperature anomalies. Finally, the large-scale atmospheric circulation processes, which triggered strong positive temperature anomalies over the region, are analyzed. This will help us to identify whether the large-scale circulation favored advective or radiative processes in producing large positive temperature anomalies.

Therefore, the warmest March of 2023 induced an extremely high number of days with extremely warm nighttime and daytime temperatures over Armenia.

We analyze composite maps and 3-day backward trajectories for March 2023 using ERA5 to understand the large-scale atmospheric circulation processes and the pathways of the warm air masses that triggered exceptionally high temperatures over Armenia (Fig. 6a–c and Supplementary Fig. 4a, b). The distinctive feature of the March 2023 composite 500 hPa geopotential height (Z500) map is the presence of two regions with exceptionally large Z500 anomalies, exceeding two standard deviations, located over the Azores and Central Asia, respectively (Fig. 6a). The subtropical ridges extended northward over the two areas of significant anticycloniccirculation.Accordingly,mean sea-levelpressure(MSLP)forMarch 2023 shows the presence of two high-pressure areas, corresponding to the Azores anticyclone (along 35°N) and a high-pressure area over Central Asia (along 50°N), respectively (Supplementary Fig. 4a). A wide low-pressure area extended to the north of the anticyclones, from the North Atlantic to the Barents Sea. This low-pressure area produced strong negative anomalies inmeanZ500andMSLP.ThecombinationofadeepNorthAtlanticcyclone and the Azores anticyclone enhanced the transportation of maritime airmass from the Atlantic to Europe, producing positive anomalies in specific humidity at the 700 hPa level in the mid-latitudes over Europe (Supplementary Fig. 4b). However, the blocking high-pressure area over Central Asia weakened zonal flow over Armenia and the South Caucasus, favoring meridional(southwesterly) flowwhichbroughtmoistsubtropicalairmasses to the region. An enhancement of meridional flow induced by blocking anticyclonic circulation has been reported previously<sup>38</sup> . 

The anticyclonic circulation over the Azores and Central Asia induced widespread positive temperature anomalies exceeding two standard deviations in the lower troposphere (at 850 hPa level) over the mid-latitudes of Europe and Central Asia (Fig. 6b). Indeed, much of Europe reported positive temperature anomalies in March 2023, except Iceland and Fennoscandia<sup>8</sup> , which were affected by negative anomalies and cyclonic circulation. The strongest temperature anomalies exceeding 7 °C were observed over Central Asia. This can be explained by the impact of anticyclonic circulation producing high temperatures due to strong radiative forcing and the warming effect associated with subsidence<sup>10,35,38,39</sup> . Satellite observations further show positive anomalies in monthly mean sunshine duration exceeding 2 h over the eastern South Caucasus (Supplementary Fig. 5a). The analysis of 3-day backward trajectories (see “The calculation of backward trajectories” in “Methods”) aligns with the large-scale atmosphericcirculationpatterns,showingthat inmost casesairmassesarrived in 

Armenia either from the southwest or south (Fig. 6c). Therefore, southwesterly and southerly winds carried air parcels that originated over the Middle East and the Eastern Mediterranean. 

In March 2023, temperatures were warmer than the 95th percentile values over Armenia throughout the troposphere, while specific humidity was extremely high in the lower and mid-troposphere (Supplementary Fig. 6a, b). Furthermore, the study area was affected by more pronounced meridional winds, while the vertical profile of zonal wind was lower compared to the median profile (Supplementary Fig. 6c, d). 

Overall, the analysis of large-scale atmospheric circulation and 3-day backward trajectories for March 2023 based on ERA5 reanalysis suggests that southwesterly and southerly winds brought extremely warm and moist air over Armenia in March 2023. Therefore, the exceptionally high temperatures in March 2023 over Armenia were mainly driven by advective processes. 

The warmest November on record. Armenia recorded another warmest month on record in November 2023<sup>13</sup> . In situ observations show asymmetric temperature anomalies across Armenia, indicating stronger anomalies in daytime temperatures compared to those for nighttime temperatures (Fig. 3a, b). This could be induced by radiative processes leading to daytime heating. In November 2023, the median anomalies in percentages of cold nights and cool days were around −10% at most of the stations (Fig. 7a, b). The median values of anomalies of warm nights and hot days consisted of 22 and 34%, respectively (Fig. 7c, d). The stronger anomalies in the percentages of hot days compared to those for warm nights further suggest the presence of diurnal asymmetries in temperature anomalies in November 2023. 

The spatial pattern of nighttime temperature anomalies (Tmin) shows relatively homogeneous distribution across Armenia with values ranging mostly between 2.5 and 3.5 °C, while some of the stations in the northeast and southeast showed positive temperature anomalies exceeding two standard deviations (Supplementary Fig. 7a). However, daytime temperatures showed stronger anomalies exceeding 4 °C in low-elevated areas (below 1000–1200 m a.s.l.), whereas mountain stations recorded weaker temperature anomalies ranging between 2 and 3 °C (Supplementary Fig. 7b). We further note that exceptionally large daytime temperature anomalies in the southeastern and northeastern parts of Armenia (reaching 5.6–6.1 °C) can be partly driven by positive anomalies of sunshine duration observed by EUMETSAT CM SAF (Supplementary Fig. 5b). Overall, in situ observationssuggestthatthediurnalasymmetriesintemperatureanomalies in November 2023 across Armenia were greatly due to stronger anomalies in daytime temperatures over low-elevated areas. 

Snowpack is not significant in Armenia during November except in high-mountain areas. The median values of monthly mean snow cover height for November over the period 1979–2024 do not reach 5 cm at most of the mountainstations located below 2200 ma.s.l. (not shown).Therefore, we considered only the high-mountain Aragats station (>3000 m a.s.l.) for analyzing snow cover depth in November 2023. Despite the ample amount of precipitation in November 2023 exceeding the upper quartile, monthly mean snow cover height was about 20 cm, which was lower than the 25th percentile value over the period 1979–2024 (Supplementary Fig. 8). Monthly precipitation and temperature were close to the norm in the previous month (October 2023)<sup>40</sup> and thus could not significantly impact snow cover in November. Therefore, the positive temperature anomalies were mainly responsible for significantly reduced snowpack over high mountain areas of Armenia in November 2023.

## Discussion 

Climate conditions in 2023 have been considered as exceptionally warm over the globe despite the recent global warming<sup>7</sup> , while climate simulations performed by Min<sup>3</sup> suggest that the heat observed in 2023 can be explained by anthropogenic warming. Strong positive temperature anomalies affected over much of Europe, the South Caucasus, and the Middle East regions in 2023<sup>2</sup> . Armenia reported its third warmest year since 1935, while some months were the warmest on record in 2023<sup>13</sup> . Exceptionally high temperatures in 2023 had a pronounced impact on Armenia, a mountainous country experiencing severe effects of climate change. Furthermore, the country has recently experienced a range of climate hazards and anomalies<sup>23–25,28–31</sup> . This study shows that warming rates in Armenia are comparable to those over Europe during recent decades. The number of stations recording exceptionally large positive temperature anomalies has dramatically increased since the mid-2000s. The frequency of large-area positive thermal anomalies has also increased in Europe over recent decades<sup>43</sup> . An extremely high number of warm nights and hot days were observed in March, November, and December 2023. Mountain topography can both dampen and enhance temperature anomalies across Armenia. Nighttime positive temperature anomalies were reduced by nocturnal coldair pools over AraratValley, while radiative forcinglikely producedstronger daytime temperature anomalies over lower elevated areas. Anomalously warm temperatures induced extremely low mountain snowpack over Armenia in March, November, and December 2023. In most cases, the observed monthly mean snow cover depth was lower than 10 cm up to 2200 m a.s.l., while in high mountain areas (above 3000 m a.s.l.) mean snow depth did not exceed 20 cm. However, we note that snowpack depends not only on the temperature of a given month, but also on precipitation totals, precipitation phase, and the thermal-precipitation conditions during the preceding period. For instance, the preceding warm and dry winter season may have contributed to the very scarce snow cover conditions observed in March 2023, while the exceptionally warm conditions in November 2023 mayhavecontributedtothereducedsnowpackobservedinDecember2023. Recent studies suggest that climate change has induced a substantial reduction in seasonal mountain snow coverglobally<sup>17,44</sup> . Seasonal snowpack 

plays a fundamental role in the formation of water resources and the water cycle over inland mountain areas, such as Armenia. It is worth noting that Armenia and its neighboring countries experience high water stress levels under present climate conditions, while future climate change is expected to further increase water scarcity in this region<sup>45,46</sup> . Lake Sevan, the largest freshwater body in the Caucasus region, is facing growing threats from climate change<sup>47,48</sup> . Furthermore, winter tourism is highly dependent on snow cover, temperature, and precipitation conditions in Armenia and other mountain countries<sup>49,50</sup> . Exceptionally high temperatures observed during the period November-March are very unfavorable for outdoor winter tourism activities in Armenia, where snowmaking practice is not widely used. 

The analysis of ERA5 composite maps, backward trajectories and vertical profiles above Armenia allowed us to identify large-scale atmospheric circulation processes, which triggered exceptional positive anomalies over the study area. We found that advective processes were dominant large-scale factors in producing strong positive temperature anomalies in March, November and December 2023. Anomalously warm temperatures were observed throughout the entire depth of the troposphere above Armenia. This is consistent with previous studies showing that wintertime warm spells have mainly been associated with the advection of warm air<sup>38,41</sup> . However, the atmospheric circulation pattern in March 2023 differed from that in November and December 2023. In March 2023, the blocking highpressure areaover Central Asia induced meridional flow that brought warm and moist subtropical air masses over Armenia and the South Caucasus from the Eastern Mediterranean and Middle East. This meridional warmair advection produced exceptionally high nighttime temperatures across Armenia. In November and December 2023, the large-scale circulation was characterized by the well-defined zonal circulation over the mid-latitudes of Europe, the South Caucasus, and Central Asia. Westerly flows advected warm and moist air masses towards Armenia. Similarly, westerly flows produced prolonged warm and wet winter weather over Europe<sup>41</sup> . Furthermore, we found that the wide anticyclonic circulation extending over the mid-latitudes, from the Azores to Central Asia, favored the formation of exceptional positive temperature anomalies over the study area during March, November and December 2023. This large-scale configuration appears to effectively block the advection of cold air from high latitudes to mid-latitudes, reducing the likelihood of the occurrence of cold air outbreaks<sup>42</sup> . The analysis of backward trajectories further supports this finding, indicating that air masses transported from the north made little or no contribution. The development of a large subtropical ridge over the Mediterranean area and Central Europe has been found to produce wintertimewarm spells over the Apennines<sup>35</sup> . Overall, our findingsare expected to be helpful in forecasting exceptionally strong positive temperature anomalies and in better understanding their impacts over the study region on monthly to seasonal timescales. However, future studies should focus on quantifying the relative contributions of advective, adiabatic, and diabatic processes to improve our mechanistic understanding of the drivers of such exceptional temperature anomalies over the study region<sup>51</sup> . 

## Methods 

Data. This study uses daily maximum (Tmax), minimum (Tmin), and mean (Tmean) temperature observations derived from 38 meteorological stations across Armenia located at altitudes ranging from 451 to 3227 m a.s.l. (Supplementary Fig. 1). The analyzed time period is 1979–2024. Daily precipitation and snow depth observations were used from the nine mountain stations located between 1700 and 2200 m a.s.l. and from Aragats high mountain station situated at 3229 m a.s.l. excluded through manual checking and automated quality control algorithms included in the CLIMATOL R package<sup>52</sup> . The observational data at the selected meteorological stations were not affected by site migration and instrument replacements. The fifth generation of European Reanalysis (ERA5)<sup>53</sup> produced by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts was used to produce composite maps and vertical profiles for March, November and December 2023. ERA5 reanalysis was downloaded from the Copernicus Climate Change Service Climate Data Store (https:// cds.climate.copernicus.eu) over the period 1979–2024 at 0.25° and onehourly spatial and temporal resolutions, respectively. The third edition of the Surface Solar Radiation Data Set - Heliosat (SARAH-3)<sup>54</sup> products from EUMETSAT’s Satellite Application Facility on Climate Monitoring (CM SAF) were used. Sunshine duration from SARAH-3 products was used over the period 1983–2024 at 0.05° spatial resolution. 

Temperature and ERA5 anomalies. Anomalies presented throughout the paper are calculated with respect to 1979–2024, except for sunshine durationfromSARAH-3(1983–2024).The absolutevaluesofanomaliesin observedmean,maximum,andminimumtemperaturesandERA5climate variables that exceeded two standard deviations, calculated from the 1979–2024 climatology, were considered as exceptionally large<sup>43</sup> (either positive or negative). 

Extreme temperature indices. The percentile-based extreme temperatureindiceswereestimated.The selectedextreme temperatureindicesused in this study are cold nights (TN10p), cool days (TX10p), warm nights (TN90p), and hot days (TX90p) as defined in the Climpact software package (www.climpact-sci.org). The four extreme temperature indices were calculated using in situ observations. Anomalies in the extreme temperature indices are calculated with respect to 1979–2024. 

The calculation of backward trajectories. The calculation of air parcel trajectories has been performed using Lagrangian techniques<sup>55</sup> . The starting position was selected at Yerevan (44.503° E; 40.177°N). The trajectoriesarecalculated from the startingposition 72 h (3 days)backward in time using one-hourly ERA5 surface and pressure-level data. The backward trajectories are initialized at 18:00 UTC each day in March, November and December 2023 at 10, 30, 50 and 100 hPa above the surface over the starting position. The different initialization heights are used to sample a near-surface layer that is assumed to be well-mixed<sup>51</sup> .
