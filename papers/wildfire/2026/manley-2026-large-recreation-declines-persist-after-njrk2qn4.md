# Large Recreation Declines Persist after Severe Wildfire but Not after Low-Severity or Prescribed Fire

## Abstract

Public lands recreation supports the well-being of millions of Americans across the Western U.S., where wildfire is intensifying and prescribed fire is expanding.Yet how these fires reshape recreation has remained poorly quantified.We analyze 185 wildfires and 54 prescribed fire sites across California and Colorado, to produce the first landscape-scale, causally identified estimates of fire's effect on recreation over time.We combined over 11 million digital-mobility observations with recreational drivers in models to estimate monthly visitation at burned and matched unburned sites within a difference-in-differences event-study framework.Effects depended on fire severity, ecological setting, and management context.Wildfires reduced visitation by 8-18% in Year 1, with losses reaching -44% by year 5 at high-severity forest sites in California, while grasslands and low-severity fires produce little to no change.Prescribed fires produce smaller, short-lived effects, even increasing recreation in Colorado.High-recreation counties were disproportionately affected by wildfires in Colorado.These findings identify recreational benefits of proactive fire management, provide a framework for evaluating recreational outcomes of fuel treatments and ecological restoration, and show how proactive management can protect recreational landscapes.
