# Large-scale modelled impacts of the 2025 Canadian wildfires

## Abstract

Canadian wildfire events have emerged as a major source of emissions in recent years, with atmospheric impacts extending far beyond the source region. In support of the State of Wildfires 2025-26 report, in this work we investigate the large-scale atmospheric effects of the 2025 Canadian wildfire season using a state-of-the-art Earth system model. We find that wildfire emissions generated substantial aerosol optical depth (AOD) increases over central Canada, with smoke transported across eastern Canada, eastern United States (US) and the North Atlantic (NA), leading to reduced net downward radiative flux, increased cloud cover and decreased precipitation over large areas downwind of the source region. Modelled surface cooling was observed across eastern Canada and eastern US, with an average surface atmospheric (2 meters) temperature difference of -0.66 °C ± 1.02 °C (one standard deviation) and local differences approaching -1.69 °C, while comparison with NCEP/NCAR reanalysis data revealed partial agreement. These findings are consistent with our previous analysis of the 2023 Canadian wildfire event impacts, meaning that such wildfire events may become an increasingly important periodic climate forcing with implications for regional weather, ecosystem functioning and broader climate feedback.
