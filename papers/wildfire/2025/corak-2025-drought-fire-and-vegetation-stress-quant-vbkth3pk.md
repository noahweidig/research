# Drought, Fire, and Vegetation Stress: Quantifying Ecosystem Responses to Extreme Events

## Abstract

Extreme events like drought and wildfire are becoming more frequent and intense, threatening agriculture and naturally vegetated ecosystems around the globe. Drought and fire directly impact the land’s ability to store and move carbon and water by altering land cover and disrupting vegetation life cycles (i.e. phenology). Understanding how extreme events affect the role of vegetation in local to global carbon and water cycles is a critical need for predicting how the land surface will respond to future changes in climate and for managing land resources under uncertain weather conditions. My dissertation work addresses this gap by exploring how feedbacks among atmospheric conditions, water availability, and plant phenology shape ecosystem responses to drought and fire. The research approach is to identify climatic factors and physical mechanisms driving ecosystem change and to quantify their associated uncertainties using a combination of field measurements, satellite remote sensing techniques, and physically-based land-surface hydrology modeling. Results are presented from studies that: 1) investigate how and why plant growth and photosynthesis were disrupted during the 2012 Flash Drought in the Midwest US, 2) develop a continental-scale, high-resolution atmospheric aridity dataset to assess drought conditions and evaluate plant water stress, 3) explore the interplay of wildfire, land cover change, and climate in a Florida wetland with regularly occurring wildfires, and 4) evaluate variability in vegetation regrowth patterns after fire events using satellite observations and ground-based measurements. Results demonstrate how high atmospheric aridity intensifies drought-induced plant water stress, increases fire risk, and reduces vegetation growth and carbon sequestration by up to 50%. This research advances the ability to predict vegetation-atmosphere interactions in response to droughts and fire, improving understanding of ecosystem resilience and informing land management strategies aimed at mitigating climate risks.
